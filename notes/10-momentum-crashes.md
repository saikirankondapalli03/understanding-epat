## The fact

Most momentum books in this lecture **die in 2008–2009**, both commodities and stocks. That is not a coincidence and it is not “because they had market beta.” CS is already long–short.

## The mechanism (Daniel et al.)

After a **market crash**:

1. The names you are **short** are the previous dogs — beaten-up, high-beta, distressed.
2. When the panic **ends**, those names **rebound the hardest**.
3. Your short leg **explodes**. The long leg (yesterday’s quality winners) lags the bounce.
4. The long–short spread **crashes**.

Researchers call this a **momentum crash**.

It can last a very long time. After **1929**, a prototype momentum strategy’s drawdown lasted on the order of **30 years**.

## A real trend index, not just Chan’s backtest (slides 86–88)

**Diversified Trends Indicator (SPDTP).** Tracked by mutual fund **RYMFX** and ETF **WDTI**.

Same break you saw in the CS stock table: fine into the crisis, **broken in the aftermath**.

## How to talk about this in an exam

- Poor 2008–2009 performance of **CS commodities** (slide 77) and **CS stocks** (slide 82) share one name: momentum crash.
- It is driven by the **short side bouncing**, not by “we forgot to hedge the market.”
- Trend-following futures indexes showed the same post-crisis hole.
- This is listed again as a **structural disadvantage** in file 14.

## Check yourself

1. A market-neutral momentum book can still blow up after a crash. How?
2. Who suffers more in the rebound: yesterday’s winners or yesterday’s losers (that you were short)?

Answers: (1) Shorts squeeze / rebound. (2) Yesterday’s losers bounce more — that is the pain.
